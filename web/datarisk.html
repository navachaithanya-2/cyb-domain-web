Data Centric Risk

Data-centric risk is an approach to cybersecurity and risk management that focuses specifically on the data itself—its sensitivity, location, flow, and exposure—rather than just securing the networks, servers, or perimeters that hold it.

As data moves freely between personal devices, remote workers, cloud platforms, and third-party vendors, the traditional network perimeter no longer suffices. A data-centric risk model assumes the perimeter may be breached and works outward from the most critical asset: the data.

  • Core Drivers of Data-Centric Risk

    • Data Sprawl
    • Over-Priviliged Access
    • Loss os Perimeter Control
    • Targeted Exfiltration

  • Key Components of Data-Centric Risk Management

    • Discovery & Classification
    • Data Lineage & Flow Tracking
    • Context-Aware Access Control
    • Protection at the Data Level
    • Continuous Monitoring & DLP

Data-Flow Map

A data flow map (often used interchangeably with a Data Flow Diagram or Data Mapping) is a visual representation of how information moves through an organization, application, or system.

It tracks data from the moment it is collected or ingested, through where it is processed and stored, all the way to how it is shared, exported, or deleted.
