3rd Party Risks

Third-party risk (often managed under Third-Party Risk Management, or TPRM) refers to the potential threats, vulnerabilities, and disruptions introduced to an organization through its relationships with external entities—such as vendors, suppliers, SaaS providers, contractors, and partners.

Even if an organization’s internal defenses are strong, a breach or failure at a trusted vendor can directly compromise the primary organization.

  1. Categories of Third-Party Risks

    • Cybersecurity & Data Risks
    • Operational Risk
    • Compliance & Legal Risk
    • Reputational Risk
    • Financial Risk

  2. The Third-Party Risk Lifecycle

    1. Sourcing & Due Diligence
    2. Contracting & SLA Design
    3. Onboarding & Access Provisioning
    4. Continuous Monitoring
    5. Offboarding & Termination

  3. Fourth-Party (Nth-Party) Risk

    4. A major challenge in modern supply chains is fourth-party risk—the risks stemming from your vendor's own sub-processors and subcontractors.

    A compromise in a widely shared open-source library, cloud provider, or API utilized by your direct vendor can cascade upstream into your systems, as seen in major supply-chain attacks.

  4. Key Mitigation Controls

    • Zero Trust Architecture
    • Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP)
    • Security Ratings & Automated Scanners
