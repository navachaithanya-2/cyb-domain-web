Physical Security

Physical security in cybersecurity refers to the protection of personnel, hardware, networks, and data from physical actions and events that could cause serious loss or damage.

Even the most advanced firewalls, encryption standards, and intrusion detection systems can be bypassed if an unauthorized individual can physically touch a server, workstation, or network switch.

The Defense-in-Depth Model for Physical Security

  • Perimeter
  • Facility Access
  • Internal Areas
  • Critical Rooms

Key Threats to Address

  • Tailgating / Piggybacking

    • An unauthorized person slipping through a secure door right behind an authorized employee.

  • Direct Hardware Tampering

    • An attacker plugging a malicious USB device into a machine or attaching a rogue network tap to a physical switch.

  • Theft of Endpoints & Media

    • Stealing unattended laptops, backup tapes, external hard drives, or discarded paperwork containing credentials or personal data.

  • Environmental Hazards

    • Failures in heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC), water leaks, or electrical power surges that cause hardware failures or data loss.

Core Physical Security Controls

  • Access Control Systems
  • Video Surveillance (CCTV)
  • Mantraps / Air-locks
  • Environmental Controls
  • Secure Disposal
