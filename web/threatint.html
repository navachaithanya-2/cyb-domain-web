Threat Intelligence

Threat Intelligence (often referred to as Cyber Threat Intelligence, or CTI) is evidence-based knowledge—including context, mechanisms, indicators, implications, and actionable advice—about existing or emerging hazards to digital assets.

Its primary purpose is to help organizations transition from purely reactive firefighting to proactive, informed defense by understanding who their adversaries are, how they operate, and what they are targeting.

The Threat Intelligence Lifecycle

  1. Planning & Direction
  2. Collection
  3. Processing
  4. Analysis
  5. Dissemination
  6. Feedback

The Three Levels Of Threat Intelligence

  • Tactical
  • Operational
  • Strategic

Common Frameworks & Data Standards

  • The Pyramid of Pain (David Bianco)
  • STIX & TAXII
  • MITRE ATT&CK
  • The Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis

  • Internal Threat Intelligence

    • Internal Threat Intelligence is generated from data produced by your own infrastructure. It represents ground-truth observations of activity within your enterprise boundary

    • Primary Sources

      • Endpoint Telemetry (EDR/XDR)
      • Network & Gateway Logs
      • Authentication & Identity Systems
      • Internal Decoys (Deception Tech)

    • Key Advantages

      • Contextual accuracy: Identifies which specific internal assets are affected.
      • Captures customized attacks or insider threats tailored specifically against your environment.

  • External Threat Intelligence

    • External Threat Intelligence is collected from the global cyber ecosystem to understand adversary capabilities, motives, and targets before they strike your network.

    • Primary Sources

      • Commercial Feeds & Threat Intel Platforms (TIPs)
      • Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT)
      • Information Sharing & Analysis Centers (ISACs)
      • Adversary Surveillance & Dark Web Monitoring

    • Key Advantages

      • Early warning: Allows defensive teams to block infrastructure before campaigns target them.
      • Strategic awareness: Informs patch prioritization (e.g., tracking which vulnerabilities are actively being exploited in the wild).
